# Post-Unblinding Results

## Main result

The first blind external comparison shows **partial prospective structural and temporal correspondence with informative disagreement**.

The systems are not interchangeable.

Their outputs nevertheless become active around some of the same temporal regions of the externally originated runtime.

## Prospective candidate correspondence

At the immediately prior frozen Aperture horizon:

**9 of 11 PsiGuard interventions (81.8%)**

occur after Aperture candidate state was already active.

This is the strongest broad pre-action correspondence in this trajectory.

Candidate state is also frequent during PsiGuard holding turns, so this result is not sufficient to treat candidate status as a calibrated intervention detector.

## Temporary formal-boundary correspondence

The most notable pre-action temporal pairs are:

- `T157 → T158`
- `T205 → T206`

where PsiGuard acted one source turn after a frozen temporary formal-boundary issuance.

Additional two-turn relationships occur at:

- `T170 → T172`
- `T226 → T228`

These episodes are scientifically interesting because the formal-boundary state was generated before comparator disclosure and before the later PsiGuard action.

They remain horizon-sensitive and are not converted into permanent exits.

## Synchronous correspondence at T196

`T196` is the strongest same-turn cross-system event.

The frozen T196 Aperture prefix:

- was `Collapse`;
- had candidate state active;
- temporarily issued formal `t*=T196`.

PsiGuard intervened during that same source turn.

Candidate state was already active at T195.

Because PsiGuard changes the generation mid-answer, the exact T196 formal state is not described as a clean advance prediction. It is a strong synchronous structural correspondence.

## Disagreement at T151

PsiGuard intervened at T151.

Aperture had no candidate state and no formal-boundary state at T150 or T151.

This is a genuine PsiGuard-only action at the registered comparison scale.

It is preserved rather than post-hoc explained away.

## Source/topology correspondence

PsiGuard's `no_reading` turns:

`T21, T116, T209, T210`

exactly match Aperture's four incomplete assistant events.

This validates the source-turn topology used for comparison and confirms that both systems are referring to the same underlying runtime record.

## Interpretation

The result is consistent with:

> **two independently developed instruments responding to related but non-identical properties of the same evolving long-horizon trajectory.**

This is stronger than a purely self-referential Aperture demonstration.

It is weaker than general scientific validation and requires replication.
